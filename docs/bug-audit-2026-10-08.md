Findings below distinguish isolated reproductions from source-level findings. P0 means immediate financial exposure; P1 means high-impact correctness/security; P2 means a narrower functional defect.

## Findings

### 1. P0 — Unpaid cash-on-delivery orders mint spendable wallet credit

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:1227`, `server/storefront/views.py:900`.

Direct checkout accepts cash on delivery and marks the order `ordered=True`. Customer cancellation interprets that flag as payment completion and credits the entire total, without checking payment type or collected funds. A customer can repeatedly create unpaid COD orders, cancel them, and spend the resulting wallet balance on other purchases. Confirmed by a probe: the wallet increased by the full total of an unpaid order.

**Fix:** Separate order placement from payment settlement. Only reverse actual captured funds, and reject unsupported COD paths. Apply the same paid-funds guard to refund approval: COD original-payment refunds merely record a reversal, while COD store-credit refunds currently issue real wallet credit.

### 2. P1 — Cash-on-delivery checkout unlocks digital assets without payment

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:1227`, `server/storefront/views.py:1173`, `server/storefront/views.py:658`.

Digital-only carts also accept COD. Completion sets `confirmed` and `ordered=True`; those are the download endpoint's payment gates. Confirmed by creating a stored digital file, checking out using default COD, then downloading its contents with HTTP 200 without any wallet debit or Stripe capture.

**Fix:** Require a settled payment for digital fulfillment and explicitly disallow COD for digital goods.

### 3. P1 — A pending refund can be approved after cancellation, paying twice

**Source:** `server/payment/services.py:102`, `server/storefront/views.py:869`.

Refund approval locks the order but only validates the refund record's status. Cancellation leaves pending refunds intact. Request a return, cancel the order, then approve the original return: approval issues a second credit and restocks the same items again. Confirmed: the second approval added another $8,500 and increased stock from its original five units to six.

**Fix:** Validate the order state and remaining refundable payment under the same order lock. Cancellation must close pending refunds; approval must refuse cancelled orders and cap cumulative refunds.

### 4. P1 — Merchant tracking can bypass cancellation/refund workflows

**Source:** `server/store/views.py:337`.

Any seller participating in an order can submit any model status, including `cancelled` or `refunded`. The handler assigns the status directly and skips payment reversal, restocking, and earnings reversal. Confirmed: a seller PATCH set an order to cancelled with stock still reduced. For multi-store orders, one seller can also change the global status of all sellers' items.

**Fix:** Restrict tracking to valid fulfillment transitions and use the canonical transition service where appropriate. Model fulfillment per store/shipment for mixed-store orders.

### 5. P1 — Staff split-tender cancellation requests too much from Stripe

**Source:** `server/payment/services.py:338`.

The cancellation service calls `call_stripe_refund` without an amount, so it requests `order.total` from Stripe, then separately credits the wallet-funded share. Stripe only captured the card share. Confirmed with mocked Stripe: an order funded partly by $50 of wallet credit requested the full order total from Stripe and issued the $50 credit. A real provider can reject this, rolling back cancellation.

**Fix:** Refund only the captured card amount remaining after previous refunds and separately restore the wallet portion.

### 6. P1 — Coupon orders can receive refunds exceeding their payment

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:829`.

Refund amounts use snapshot unit prices, which are before the checkout coupon, rather than allocated amounts actually paid. Confirmed: a 50%-off order requested an $8,500 merchandise refund exceeding the entire order total. Store-credit approval can issue that amount; original-payment refunds can fail at the provider. Tax allocations are also absent.

**Fix:** Persist final paid amounts and discount/tax allocation per order line, and calculate refunds from the remaining paid balance.

### 7. P1 — Merchant earnings ignore checkout coupons

**Source:** `server/store/services.py:198`, `server/storefront/views.py:1175`.

Merchant sale entries sum snapshot prices without allocating the checkout coupon. Confirmed in the coupon probe: merchandise billed at 50% off still generated $8,500 of gross merchant earnings. Unless coupons are explicitly platform funded, payout obligations exceed the merchandise collected; there is no explicit subsidy ledger here.

**Fix:** Allocate discounts by eligible store/line, or explicitly record and fund a platform subsidy according to the coupon policy.

### 8. P1 — Customer cancellation leaves merchant sale earnings withdrawable

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:881`.

The customer path restores stock and credits the buyer, but never calls `process_refund_deduction`. Confirmed: the sale entry remained unreversed after cancellation. Financial aggregation uses existing ledger entries rather than filtering cancelled orders, so those earnings can clear into payout balances after escrow.

**Fix:** Route cancellation through a shared atomic inventory/payment/ledger reversal operation.

### 9. P2 — Customer cancellation fails to restore variant inventory

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:888`.

The customer path increments aggregate stock only. Confirmed: buying and cancelling one Large option restored aggregate stock to five but left Large stock at two instead of three. Existing variant testing invokes `restore_order_inventory` directly rather than exercising this HTTP cancellation path, so it misses the bug.

**Fix:** Use the canonical restocking function, including variant quantities, from the actual endpoint.

### 10. P1 — Concurrent cancellation can refund and restock twice

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:870`, `server/core/services.py:31`.

Source-level finding. Cancellation reads the order without `select_for_update`. Two PostgreSQL transactions can both observe confirmed status before either writes; each then executes stock increments and wallet credit. The wallet helper serializes balance updates but has no unique reversal reference, so both credits still succeed. Atomic transactions alone do not prevent this.

**Fix:** Lock the order before validating state and add a durable unique cancellation/payment reversal identity. Serialize refund creation on the same order too: the claimed-products existence check at line 825 also runs without an order lock.

### 11. P1 — Concurrent top-up confirmations can double-credit one Stripe payment

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:764`, `server/core/models.py:271`.

Source-level finding. Both confirmations can pass the reference existence check before either credit occurs. The later wallet lock does not repeat that check, and transaction references are not unique. Both requests can therefore credit the same paid session.

**Fix:** Lock the wallet/user before checking the session, or create a unique processed-payment record atomically and credit only when it is newly created.

### 12. P2 — Discounts are not allocated to taxable lines in mixed carts

**Source:** `server/payment/tax.py:341`.

The local provider caps taxable merchandise by the whole discounted subtotal. It does not allocate discounts between taxable and exempt lines. Confirmed: $100 physical plus $100 exempt digital merchandise with a $150 discounted subtotal still used $100 taxable merchandise. A proportional 25% order coupon should leave $75 of taxable merchandise; conversely, a coupon restricted to the exempt line needs a different allocation.

**Fix:** Supply final discounted line totals to tax providers instead of a whole-cart subtotal cap.

### 13. P2 — Generated affiliate URLs use an unmounted route

**Source:** `server/affiliates/views.py:72`, `server/storefront/urls.py:42`.

Source-level finding. Link creation returns an absolute backend URL under `/affiliate/r/...`, while the active storefront URL configuration mounts affiliate routes under `/api/v1/affiliates/`. The legacy prefix exists only in the unused legacy URL configuration. Through the proxy, backend absolute-URI construction also generates a backend-origin link instead of the frontend's `/api/affiliates/r/...` route.

**Fix:** Build shareable links from the configured frontend origin and the actual proxy route, with validated/encoded path parameters.

### 14. P2 — Wallet deposit limits can be bypassed using multiple unpaid sessions

**Source:** `server/storefront/views.py:732`, `server/storefront/views.py:767`.

Source-level finding. Daily/monthly limits are checked against credited deposits at session creation. A customer can create many sessions before paying any, then pay and confirm them all. Confirmation checks only the per-payment maximum, so the aggregate limits are not enforced.

**Fix:** Reserve allowance under a user lock with durable funding attempts; define how already-captured over-limit payments are refunded or reviewed at confirmation.

### 15. P2 — Configured ZIP tax overrides are ignored for hard-coded exempt states

**Source:** `server/payment/tax.py:260`.

Source-level finding. The provider returns zero for AK/DE/MT/NH/OR before querying `TaxJurisdiction`. An administrator can save an active nonzero municipal/ZIP override for one of these states, but checkout will never use it. This contradicts the configurable destination-jurisdiction engine independently of what any particular jurisdiction legally requires.

**Fix:** Resolve explicit local rules first, then apply default exemption behavior only when no override exists.

## Recommended repair sequence

1. Close unpaid COD credit and digital fulfillment paths.
2. Unify customer, staff, and merchant order mutations around locked payment/inventory/ledger operations; validate outstanding refunds and cumulative reversals.
3. Add database-enforced payment idempotency and top-up allowance reservations.
4. Persist per-line paid totals and discount/tax allocation; derive refunds and merchant earnings from them.
5. Repair variant cancellation, tax override precedence, and generated affiliate links.
6. Add endpoint-level regressions and PostgreSQL concurrency tests, then rerun live suites against responsive seeded services.
